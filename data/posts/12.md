# From Dosas to Doodles: My DevConf India Story

## Day 0: Before the Conference, There Was Dinner

I reached a day early, on February 12, not to prep slides or booths but to share a meal. This was Matthew Miller's first time in India, and as someone who genuinely believes that communities are built around tables before stages, I wanted to do one thing right: food.

We were four people, sitting together at a small South Indian place, ordering masala dosas, talking to the vendor while he flipped them on the hot plate. There were questions about spices, heat levels, and what "medium spicy" actually means in India (spoiler: it still hits).

It was intimate. Calm. Unrushed.

And in hindsight, it set the tone for everything that followed.

## Day 1 Morning: The Quiet Before the Crowd

I reached the DevConf India campus around 10:00 AM, and for a brief moment, it was quiet. I was the first one at the Fedora booth. It was a nice corner spot, one of those places where people naturally stop, look around, and wander in. I didn't know it then, but I was about to barely sit down for the next six hours. They came to ask if we were the registration desk, because we were so crowded with people, but unfortunately, I had to break there thought.

Soon after, Akashdeep, Yaash, Shaunik, and other contributors joined in. We set things up together, posters, QR codes, swag and just like that, Fedora had a home in Pune, India for the next two days.

## Day 1: Six Hours, A Thousand Conversations

At 10:15 AM, conversations started.

At 4:15 PM, I finally sat down.

Somewhere in between, someone casually told me:

> "Bro, you've spoken to at least 1000–1200 people today. Please sit."

And honestly? I didn't even notice.

There were:

- College students discovering Fedora for the first time
- Open source contributors from other projects
- Enterprise folks asking serious questions
- People who recognized me and said, "Oh you're the release engineering person, right?"
  
  Ngl that made my day

That one always makes me smile.

We spoke about:

- What Fedora stands for
- How Fedora releases actually work
- Why Fedora and Ubuntu feel different
- How security, customization, and upstream-first development matter

Some conversations turned to deep community-driven, cybersecurity, enterprise workflows, and long-term support. That's where I pointed folks to Red Hat Developer Sandboxes and other AI related usecases which Red Hat is working on, showing how open source and enterprise don't compete—they complement each other.

## Cross-Community Magic (My Favorite Part)

One of the best things about this booth was who showed up.

- Folks from React Hyderabad talked about building more APAC-friendly community collaborations
- Someone from a FreeBSD background wanted to understand Fedora's release model
- From the campus itself, I was approached by faculty and GDG group to promote open-source guest lectures for getting them involved with Fedora
- Others just wanted to know how communities scale without losing soul

These weren't transactional chats. These were "let's do something together" conversations.

And those are the ones I'm most excited to follow up on in the coming weeks.

## The Quiz: Learning, But Make It Fun

A week before the conference, I had an idea. I asked Akashdeep if we could introduce a quiz at the booth, not to test people, but to invite them in.

No trick questions. No rocket science. Just:

- What is Fedora?
- What does it stand for?
- How does the community work?

We arranged the swag bottles, thanks to Akashdeep for taking care of the logistics, and suddenly the booth became even busier.

By the end:

- ~120 quiz responses
- Top scorers entered into a raffle
- 4 winners each day, chosen by spinning the wheel

Watching people learn while smiling—that was a win.

## Day 2: Same Energy, New Depth

Day 2 started strong and stayed packed till around 3:30 PM. Many people came back—with:

- Follow-up questions
- GSoC ideas
- "I checked Fedora last night" stories

I also attended Matthew's talk: **"30 Fedora Releases in 30 Minutes."**

Seeing Fedora's journey laid out like that—while realizing I've been part of recent releases and am heading towards Fedora 44—hit differently. It was grounding. Motivating. A quiet reminder that this work matters.

🖍 Doodle idea: A timeline with Fedora version numbers marching forward.

## Food, Care, and Community (Even When I Wasn't There)

Day 1 ended with dinner together—a simple but much-needed decompression. For Day 2, I had planned something special: dinner at Ishara, a restaurant that empowers specially-abled staff while serving incredible Indian food.

Unfortunately, I caught a fever and couldn't make it—but the team went, and knowing that they experienced it still made me happy.

Community is also about trusting others to carry the moment forward.

## Walking Away (And Needing a Week to Recover)

DevConf India was just two days.

But honestly?

I needed a week to recover from the energy, the conversations, the joy, and the sheer human connection.

This event reinforced something I deeply believe in:

- Fedora in APAC matters
- Community presence matters
- Listening matters more than talking

I'm now following up on connections, helping new contributors find their first SIGs, and figuring out how to turn all this energy into long-term impact.

## A Thank You (And an Open Invitation)

To everyone who stopped by the Fedora booth.

To those who asked questions.

To those who clicked photos and tagged me on LinkedIn.

To those who said, "I want to start contributing."

Thank you.

If you're reading this and wondering whether you belong in open source, the answer is yes. And Fedora will always have space for you.
